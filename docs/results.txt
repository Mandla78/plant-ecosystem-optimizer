--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE SCORING FUNCTION, SETTLED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The engine prints its own statistics in the evaluation log, which settles what
we spent most of the event guessing:

    main_score = entropy * density          (so ALPHA = 1)
    score      = 0.8 * main_score + 0.2 * longevity_score
    leaderboard = score * 1e9

    entropy = -SUM p_i * log_31(p_i)   over the final plant counts
    density = total_plants_planted_C / (rows * cols)

tests/test_ground_truth.py asserts all four of these against the real logs.

HOW WE GOT ALPHA WRONG FIRST: with only the Level 1 score to go on, we solved
0.8*H*(C/Cmax)^a + 0.2*L = score for `a` and got 1.9913, which looked convincing
to four significant figures. It was wrong, because it assumed our SIMULATED
garden (5 species, H=0.4687) matched the real one (4 species, H=0.3463). A
calibration is only as good as the state you assume produced it. One data point
that fits is not evidence; the log that printed entropy and density directly is.

That mistake had a real cost: believing coverage was SQUARED, we spent hours
optimising density - which was already near its ceiling - instead of diversity,
which was where all the remaining score actually was.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE THREE THINGS THAT ACTUALLY DECIDED THE SCORE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. PLACEMENT ONTO AN OCCUPIED CELL IS DENIED, NOT REPLACED.

   The problem statement says: "If your action dictates that a plant must be
   placed on an occupied cell, then the existing plant on that cell will be
   replaced by your new plant."

   The engine log says: "Placement denied for plant 6 at (118, 113): plant
   already occupies cell".

   The specification is wrong. Every rebalancing-by-overwrite action we emitted
   did nothing. The deeper consequence is that PLANTING ORDER DECIDES OWNERSHIP:
   whoever reaches a cell first keeps it.

2. DENSITY LOOKS AFTER ITSELF. ENTROPY IS THE WHOLE GAME.

   Plantable fractions per level are 0.72 / 0.876 / 0.813 / 0.900, and we
   achieved 0.72 / 0.867 / 0.813 / 0.882 - essentially the ceiling everywhere.
   A single fast spreader fills the map whatever you do.

   Entropy, meanwhile, finished at 44-86% of its ceiling. Every remaining point
   of score was in diversity.

3. ONE SPECIES EATS THE GARDEN, AND OAK IS THE WORST OFFENDER.

       Level 3: Oak 68.8% + Razorgrass 18.8% = 87.6% of all plants
       Level 4: Razorgrass 56.9% + Oak 24.9% = 81.8%

   Oak is doubly bad. It monopolises, AND once mature it shades radius 4, and
   Grass has no_shade_survival. In our first Level 3 run Oak reached 12,001
   cells and killed every Grass plant - Grass finished on ZERO. Loamcrawlers
   requires Grass coverage >= 4%, and Loamcrawlers gates most of the unlock
   tree, so one species quietly locked us out of a 30-species level. That run
   scored 20,606,381 with 4 species.

   The fix is to deny the runaways TIME rather than space: release Oak and
   Razorgrass only in the final ~12 ticks. Oak matures at 20, so it never
   matures, never spreads and never shades - but still counts as a species.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE HALF-PERCENT THAT COST A LEVEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Level 2 finished with Lavender on 108 cells = 1.5% of the grid. The Nectaris
animal needs 2%. No Nectaris meant no Crimson Vine and no Orange Blossom, and
only 6 of 28 reachable species ever unlocked.

The cause was ordering: Grass was planted first, spread across the map, and
Lavender's later placements were refused because those cells were occupied.
Planting the slow gate-critical species FIRST and Grass last cleared all four
animal gates from hand-placed cells alone (Lavender 5.1%, Rose 6.1%, Sunflower
5.7%, Grass 7.7%).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT WE TRIED THAT DID NOT WORK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each was implemented and measured, not dismissed on theory. They are recorded
because a ruled-out branch is worth as much as a good one.

  Two-zone map (burn a work zone, keep a pristine reserve)
      FAILED. Spread does not respect our zones - fast species escaped and
      thrashed the whole map anyway.

  Small-footprint early cascade (5-25% of the map, from tick 200-340)
      FAILED. Coverage collapse dominated.

  Staggering seeds by spread speed
      FAILED as originally built, which throttled every fast species at once.
      NOTE: the same idea, narrowed to a hard release-tick for the TWO runaway
      species, is what finally worked. The mechanism was right; the first
      version applied it too broadly.

  Iterative proportional fitting of seed weights (experimental/balance.py)
      FAILED. Entropy got WORSE every round - the final mix is driven by spread,
      not by how many seeds we hand out.

  Capping seeds for fast spreaders
      FAILED. The fast spreaders ARE the density.

  Gate-push (spend the early budget only on the gate species)
      FAILED at first, then became essential once we learned that placement is
      denied on occupied cells and reordered it to plant the SLOW species first.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHERE THE REMAINING SCORE WAS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Taking Level 3 as the example: entropy 0.298 against a 0.670 ceiling for the 10
species already present. Simply balancing those ten - no new unlocks at all -
would have given

    0.8 * 0.670 * 0.813 = 0.436   ->  436M, against the 199M achieved.

The same arithmetic on Levels 2 and 4 puts a balanced version of exactly what we
already had at roughly 1.5 billion, which is where the leading entries finished.
We were not short of species or of coverage. We were short of BALANCE, and we
worked that out too late to act on it properly.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HONEST LIMITATION OF THIS REPOSITORY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The simulator in src/engine reproduces DENSITY and legality well - it predicted
one Level 3 score to within 2.4% - but it does NOT reproduce COMPOSITION. It
predicts a Grass monoculture for a Level 2 solution that really produced six
balanced species. Late decisions were therefore reasoned from the returned
evaluation logs rather than from the simulator, and the composition-dependent
unit tests skip rather than assert against a model known to be wrong on that
axis. Fixing the spread model is the first thing worth doing next.
