HOW THE TERRAIN IDS WERE DECODED  (they are not documented anywhere)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  terrain 1 = WATER. Proof: across L2/L3/L4, terrain-1 cells are adjacent to an
              unlisted cell essentially ZERO times (0, 2 and 1 occurrences out
              of many thousands of neighbours), and their boundary is always
              terrain-0 cells whose soil is CLAY. The spec says "Water Feature:
              always surrounded by clay soil types". Exact match.
  terrain 2 = STONE. Large solid blobs in L3/L4; in L1 it forms the straight
              greenhouse divider walls.
  terrain 4 = PATH. Thin, 1-cell-wide winding trails across L3/L4. Absent from
              L1 and L2.
  terrain 3 = never present in any level file (presumably the Crack id, created
              at runtime by the Earthquake in L4).
  unlisted  = default ground, dirt soil, plantable.

  Both stone and path satisfy Stone Reed's "must_be_adjacent_to: rock_or_path".
  Mire Bloom's "must_be_adjacent_to: water" means the clay ring around lakes -
  and Mire Bloom is the ONLY plant that accepts clay soil [0,1,2]. That is
  clearly deliberate: Mire Bloom is the only way to monetise the clay rings.

LEVEL 1 READING OF THAT MAP
  It is a greenhouse: 6 rectangular beds, each 9 wide x 20 tall, separated by
  stone walls at cols 10, 20, 30, 40. Everything outside cols 10-40, plus the
  band at rows 20-29, is default dirt and IS plantable - that open dirt is
  actually the majority of our usable space (1,440 cells).

  Bed contents (rows 0-19 top band, rows 30-49 bottom band):
    cols 11-19 top    : WATER channel (unplantable) + default-dirt core
    cols 21-29 top    : mud   (plantable)
    cols 31-39 top    : clay  (DEAD - no starting plant accepts clay)
    cols 11-19 bottom : clay  (DEAD)
    cols 21-29 bottom : mud   (plantable)
    cols 31-39 bottom : WATER channel + default-dirt core

  So of the 2,500 cells: 1,800 are plantable, 360 are clay we cannot use with
  the 5 starting species, and 340 are water/stone.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LEVEL 2 / 3 / 4 SHAPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  L2 (70x100): open dirt field, 88% plantable. A handful of lakes (595 water
     cells) with clay rings, and a small amount of stone. No paths.
     The most forgiving level - big enough for real spreading, small enough
     that a 5% coverage gate is only 350 cells.

  L3 (150x150): open terrain with several large lakes, big stone outcrops and
     winding paths. 18,295 plantable. 5% coverage = 1,125 cells, which is
     already beyond what we can hand-plant, so natural spread must carry it.

  L4 (200x300): the big one. 54,012 plantable, and 5% coverage = 3,000 cells.
     Total manual placements available across all 800 ticks = 16,000, i.e. only
     30% of the plantable area. L4 is won by SEED PLACEMENT GEOMETRY, not by
     brute-force planting.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SEASON TIMELINE (matters for no_winter_spread: Rose Bush, Lavender)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  L1/L2 (500 ticks): Spring 0-99, Summer 100-199, Autumn 200-299,
                     WINTER 300-399, Spring 400-499.
  L3/L4 (800 ticks): as above, then Summer 500-599, Autumn 600-699,
                     WINTER 700-799.   <-- note L3/L4 END IN WINTER.

  CONSEQUENCE: on L3 and L4 the final 100 ticks are winter, so Rose Bush and
  Lavender cannot spread during the exact window that determines the final
  state. They must be established BEFORE tick 700, or hand-planted at the end
  (hand-planting is not spreading, so it should still work).

  Orange Blossom gets spread_rate 4 in summer (a conditional_modifier) - note
  that is SLOWER than its base 3, so summer actually hurts it.
